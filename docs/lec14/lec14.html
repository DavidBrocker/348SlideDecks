When to Use Repeated Measures ANOVA

Use Repeated Measures ANOVA when:

  • You have the same participants measured more than once (e.g., pre-test/post-test).

  • You’re interested in changes over time or under different conditions.

    • Testing whether students’ enjoyment of statistics improves after receiving lollipops for 2 weeks.

Between vs. Within Subjects ANOVA

Between-subjects ANOVA

  • Compares means between groups (Group A vs. B vs. C).

  • Error comes from individual differences between people.

Within-subjects (repeated measures) ANOVA

  • Compares means within the same participants over time or conditions.

  • Controls for individual differences.

Why Repeated-Measures ANOVA?

  • Can’t use multiple paired-samples t-tests.

    • Alpha inflation increases the Type I error rate.

  • Repeated-Measures ANOVA allows us to:

    • Compare within-subject changes across time.

    • Control for individual variability.

    • Test for an overall time effect.

Within-Subjects ANOVA

Where the variance comes from

  • Between-subjects ANOVA splits variance into between-group and error (within-group) parts.

  • Within-subjects ANOVA has a third source, because each person contributes several scores:

Time
How the means vary across the time points (the effect we care about).
Subjects
How people differ from each other overall.
Error
What is left over once time and subjects are accounted for.

Within-Subjects ANOVA

Partitioning the variability

Within-Subjects ANOVA

The key idea

Removing the person

\(SS_{W} = SS_{Subjects} + SS_{Error}\)

\(SS_{Error} = SS_{W} - SS_{Subjects}\)

  • In between-subjects ANOVA, differences between people sit inside the error term.

  • In within-subjects ANOVA we subtract them out, so the error is smaller and the test is more powerful.

Calculating Repeated-Measures ANOVA

The F ratio

\(F = \dfrac{MS_{Time}}{MS_{Error}}\)

\(MS_{Time} = \dfrac{SS_{Time}}{df_{Time}} = \dfrac{SS_{Time}}{k - 1}\)

\(MS_{Error} = \dfrac{SS_{W} - SS_{Subjects}}{df_{Error}} = \dfrac{SS_{W} - SS_{Subjects}}{(n-1)(k-1)}\)

  • \(k\) is the number of time points (or conditions) and \(n\) is the number of participants.

Degrees of Freedom

Rules

  • Time: \(df_{Time} = k - 1\)

  • Subjects: \(df_{Subjects} = n - 1\)

  • Error: \(df_{Error} = (n - 1) \times (k - 1)\)

Degrees of Freedom

Example

20 students rate their stress at three time points during finals week: three days before the exam, the night before, and after the exam.

What are the df’s for a one-way repeated-measures ANOVA on Time?

Reveal answer

  • \(df_{Time} = 3 - 1 = 2\)

  • \(df_{Subjects} = 20 - 1 = 19\)

  • \(df_{Error} = 19 \times 2 = 38\)

Repeated-Measures ANOVA

Assumptions

Assumptions

  • Normality of the differences.

  • Sphericity: the variances of all pairwise differences are about equal.

    • Test: Mauchly’s Test.

  • If sphericity is violated, use the Greenhouse-Geisser correction.

    • Always check sphericity when the within-subject factor has more than two levels.

Repeated-Measures ANOVA

Effect size

Partial eta squared

\(\eta_p^2 = \dfrac{SS_{Time}}{SS_{Time} + SS_{Error}} = \dfrac{105.58}{105.58 + 44.25} =0.70\)

  • Benchmarks are the same as other ANOVAs: about .01 small, .06 medium, .14 large.

Post-hoc comparisons

  • A significant F says that some time points differ, not which.

Within-Subjects ANOVA

Reporting F

If asked to report findings in terms of the Null Hypothesis (H0), you should report findings as:

  • Reject \(H_0\), or

  • Fail to Reject \(H_0\)

  • If asked to report findings in general or for publication, you need to report \(F(df_{Time}, df_{Error}) = F, p\), an effect size, and the means and standard deviations at each time point.

Within-Subjects ANOVA

Reporting the pizza study

Productivity differed significantly across the three time points, F(2, 58) = 69.19, p < .001, \(\eta_p^2\) = 0.70. Productivity was highest 10 minutes after pizza (M = 6.93, SD = 1.61) and lowest an hour after pizza (M = 4.33, SD = 1.60); before lunch it was M = 5.14 (SD = 1.56).

Interpreting F

What F means

\(MS_{Time}\) is made up of \(MS_{Error}\) + the theoretical change over time:

  • \(MS_{Time} = \text{change over time} + MS_{Error}\)

  • If there is no change: \(MS_{Time} = 0 + MS_{Error}\), so \(F = \dfrac{MS_{Error}}{MS_{Error}} = 1\).

  • If there is a change: \(F = 1 + \dfrac{\text{change}}{MS_{Error}}\), which is greater than 1.

  • When \(F \leq 1\), it’s not likely to be significant.

Hypothesis Testing: ANOVA

Decision rule

  • \(F \leq 1\): fail to reject the null hypothesis.

  • \(F > 1\): compare to the critical value (or use the p-value). Reject \(H_0\) only if \(p < \alpha\).

Reporting F

Active-learning example

Attitudes toward statistics differed significantly across the semester, F(2, 158) = 281.96, p < .001. Attitudes were highest at the end of the semester (M = 6.27, SD = 1.69) and lowest at the beginning (M = 4.56, SD = 1.42). Mid-semester attitudes were M = 5.45 (SD = 1.59).

Summary: Within-Subjects ANOVA

  • Compares three or more related means (the same people measured repeatedly).

  • Splits the variability into time, subjects, and error, and removes the subject differences from the error term.

  • \(F = MS_{Time} / MS_{Error}\) with \(df = (k - 1,\ (n - 1)(k - 1))\).

  • Check sphericity (Mauchly’s Test) and use the Greenhouse-Geisser correction if it is violated.

  • Follow a significant \(F\) with post-hoc paired comparisons (Bonferroni).

  • Report \(F(df_{Time}, df_{Error})\), \(p\), an effect size, and the means and standard deviations.
