Analysis of Variance

ANOVA

ANOVA, like t-tests, compares the means of different groups to determine if they differ significantly from one another.

  • ANOVA can examine independent variables with more than 2 groups.

  • ANOVA can also be used for just 2 groups (then \(F = t^2\) and it gives the same answer as the t-test).

Coffee Example Vol. 2

Why not do more t-tests?

Recall that our \(\alpha\) level is set at .05. If we want to find out whether coffee affects happiness, we’d need three t-tests:

Three pairwise comparisons

  • \(t_1\): Coffee vs. Placebo
  • \(t_2\): Coffee vs. Control
  • \(t_3\): Placebo vs. Control

Why Not Do More T-Tests?

The problem

  • Each test is done independently.

  • None accounts for the shared variability across all groups.

  • By running multiple t-tests, we increase the probability of a false positive (Type I error) and ignore the combined structure of the data.

  • ANOVA solves both problems by analyzing all groups at once, partitioning total variance into between-group variance (differences due to the treatment) and within-group variance (random variation).

Why Not Do More T-Tests?

Alpha inflation

  • Each hypothesis test has a 5% chance of a false positive.

  • When we test them one-by-one, those chances accumulate.

Familywise error rate

\(\alpha_\text{FWE} = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^k\)

For three tests: \(1 - (0.95)^3 = 0.1426 \approx 14\%\)

The takeaway

ANOVA protects against this inflation by giving us one overall test of group differences (F-statistic), keeping our Type I error at the intended 5%.

Between subjects ANOVA

Why “analysis of variance”?

  • If it’s all about the means, why is it called Analysis of Variance?

  • To compare the means, we analyze two types of variance:

    • Variance within each group, around its own mean (within-group or error variance)

    • Variance between the group means (between-group variance)

The F Statistic

F Statistic
The ratio of between-group variance to within-group (error) variance. Named in honor of Sir Ronald Fisher, who developed ANOVA.

Calculating F

ANOVA Formula

\(F = \dfrac{MS_B}{MS_E} \qquad MS_B = \dfrac{SS_B}{df_B} \quad MS_E = \dfrac{SS_E}{df_E} \qquad df_B = k-1 \quad df_E = N - k\)

MS
Mean Square: a sum of squares divided by its degrees of freedom (a variance).
\(_B\) and \(_E\)
Between-group variance and within-group (Error) variance.
\(SS\), \(k\), \(N\)
Sum of Squares, number of groups, number of participants.

Calculating F

Calculating the variance

ANOVA looks at variance. To calculate the variance:

Variance

\(\Large{s^2 = \dfrac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}}\)

  • Divide by (degrees of freedom).

    • This is the variance, but in ANOVA we also call it a Mean Square (MS).

Calculating the Mean Square (MS)

  • \(MS = \dfrac{SS}{df}\)

  • Calculate the SS (sum of squared deviations).

  • Divide the SS by the degrees of freedom.

Partitioning the variance

Total = between + within

\(SS_{Total} = SS_B + SS_E\)

  • \(SS_B\) (between): how much variance comes from group differences?

    • \(SS_B = \sum n_j(\bar{x}_j - \bar{x}_{grand})^2\)

  • \(SS_E\) (error, within): how much variance is random variation within the groups?

    • \(SS_E = \sum\sum (x_{ij} - \bar{x}_j)^2\)

Degrees of Freedom

The group project

Imagine a group of 5 students is submitting a project, and the total score for the group must be 100 points combined.

  • The first 4 students can choose any score they want, say 15, 20, 25, and 10.

  • But the 5th student’s score isn’t free anymore. It must be whatever makes the total = 100. In other words, only 4 scores are “free to vary.”

  • That’s n − 1 = 4 degrees of freedom.

Degrees of Freedom

Rules for a one-way ANOVA

  • Between groups: \(df_{B} = k - 1\)

  • Within groups: \(df_E = N - k\)

  • Total: \(df_{Total} = N - 1\)

(\(k\) is the number of groups in the independent variable.)

Calculating the Mean Squares and F

The F ratio

\(MS_B = \dfrac{SS_B}{df_B} \qquad MS_E = \dfrac{SS_E}{df_E} \qquad F = \dfrac{MS_B}{MS_E}\)

By hand

\(SS_B = 100(106.00 - 101.67)^2 + 100(100.00 - 101.67)^2 + 100(99.00 - 101.67)^2 = 2866.67\)

\(MS_B = \dfrac{2866.67}{2} = 1433.33\), \(MS_E = \dfrac{66825.00}{297} = 225.00\), so \(F = \dfrac{1433.33}{225.00} = 6.37\)

Interpreting F

\(MS_B\) is made up of \(MS_E\) + the theoretical difference between groups:

  • \(MS_B = \text{group difference} + MS_E\)

  • \(MS_B = \text{group difference (0)} + MS_E\)

  • \(MS_B = MS_E\)

  • \(F = \dfrac{MS_B}{MS_E}\)

  • \(F = 1\)

Interpreting F

  • When \(F < 1\), it’s not likely to be significant.

  • When there is a group difference:

    • \(MS_B = \text{(Number > 0)} + MS_E\)

    • \(F = \dfrac{\text{Difference} + MS_E}{MS_E} = 1 + \dfrac{\text{Difference}}{MS_E}\)

    • The bigger the group difference relative to the error, the bigger \(F\) gets (larger than 1).

The F Distribution

Is our F big enough?

  • The F distribution is right-skewed and never negative.

  • In R: qf(.95, df1 = 2, df2 = 297) gives the critical value 3.03. Since \(6.37 > 3.03\), we reject \(H_0\).

Hypothesis testing: ANOVA

Decision rule

  • \(F \le 1\): fail to reject the null hypothesis.

  • \(F > 1\): compare to the critical value (or use the p-value). Reject \(H_0\) only if \(F > F_{critical}\), equivalently if \(p < \alpha\).

Assumptions of One-Way ANOVA

Assumptions

  1. The DV is continuous (interval or ratio scale).
  2. Observations are independent, and each person is in only one group.
  3. Normality: the scores (residuals) in each group are approximately normal.
  4. Homogeneity of variance: the groups have similar variances (Levene’s Test).

assumptions.R

library(car)

# Equal variances? (H0: the variances are equal)
leveneTest(iq ~ group, data = ssld)

# Normal residuals? (H0: the residuals are normal)
shapiro.test(residuals(fit))

Effect Size: Eta Squared

How much of the variance is explained by the groups?

\(\eta^2\)

\(\eta^2 = \dfrac{SS_B}{SS_{Total}} = \dfrac{2866.67}{69691.67} = 0.041\)

η² Interpretation
0.01 Small effect
0.06 Medium effect
0.14 Large effect
  • Our groups explain about 4.11% of the variance in IQ: a small-to-medium effect.

Reporting F

If asked to report findings in terms of the Null Hypothesis (H0), you should report findings as:

  • Reject \(H_0\), or

  • Fail to Reject \(H_0\)

Reporting F

If asked to report findings in general or for publication, you need to report 5 things:

  • \(F\)(df for the between group MS, df for the error MS)

  • \(F\)-value

  • \(p\)-value

  • Mean and standard deviation of each group

  • An effect size (\(\eta^2\))

Reporting F

IQ scores differed significantly across the three groups, F(2, 297) = 6.37, p = .002, \(\eta^2\) = 0.04. The SSLD group had the highest mean (M = 106.00, SD = 15.00), followed by the placebo group (M = 100.00, SD = 15.00) and the control group (M = 99.00, SD = 15.00). Tukey’s HSD showed that the SSLD group scored higher than the placebo group (p = .014) and the control group (p = .003), which did not differ from each other (p = .885).

Reporting F

There was a significant difference in IQ between the groups, F(2, 297) = 6.37, p = .002.

Identifying Degrees of Freedom

One-way ANOVA, equal groups

A researcher compares three different study methods (flashcards, rereading, and practice testing) on exam performance. There are 15 students in each group.

  • What is \(df_B\)?

    • \(k-1 = 3-1 = 2\)

  • What is \(df_E\)?

    • \(N = 15 \times 3 = 45\)

    • \(df_E = N-k = 45-3 = 42\)

Identifying Degrees of Freedom

One-way ANOVA, unequal groups

A psychologist compares four types of therapy for anxiety. Group sizes are 10, 12, 14, and 9 participants.

  • What is \(df_B\)?

    • \(k = 4\)

    • \(k-1 = 4-1 = 3\)

  • What is \(df_E\)?

    • \(N=10+12+14+9 = 45\)

    • \(df_E = N-k = 45-4 = 41\)

Summary: One-Way ANOVA

  • Compares three or more group means with one overall test.

  • \(F = MS_B / MS_E\): between-group variance relative to within-group variance.

  • Follow a significant \(F\) with a post-hoc test (Tukey’s HSD) to find which groups differ.

  • Check assumptions: independence, normality, equal variance.

  • Report \(F\)(df, df), \(p\), group means and SDs, and an effect size (\(\eta^2\)).
